==================================================
VISUAL SEARCH STRATEGY
==================================================

Inspect the ENTIRE image before generating JSON. Complete all search passes
first.

PASS 1 — TRAFFIC CONTROL
Inspect every traffic control device individually, including its mounting:
• traffic signs and supplementary plates
• regulatory, warning, guide, information, parking, mandatory,
  prohibition, priority and temporary signs
• overhead signs
• traffic, pedestrian, bicycle and bus signals, including active states
• distant and partially occluded signs

Every visually distinct device gets its own object. Never merge different
signs because they share a pole.

MANDATORY SIGN INSPECTION
Inspect EVERY object that could be a traffic sign.

Pay attention to:
• circular, triangular, rectangular and octagonal signs
• roadside boards and reflective panels
• signs on islands, medians, roadside areas and near street lights
• text, language, possible country, pictograms and symbols
• distant signs and supplementary plates

Return a sign when the board is visibly present even if its text or symbol
cannot be identified.

Use:
"text": "unreadable"
ONLY when visible text exists but cannot be read.

Use:
"symbol": "unreadable"
ONLY when a visible pictogram exists but cannot be identified.

Never merge different signs merely because they have the same shape.

PASS 2 — ROAD MARKINGS
Inspect every visually distinct painted marking:
• stop lines
• crosswalks
• straight, turn and merge arrows
• bicycle and bus symbols
• painted road text and speed markings
• chevrons and hatch markings

Keep visually different markings separate. Do NOT merge arrows with lane
boundaries.

PASS 3 — TRAFFIC INFRASTRUCTURE
Inspect:
• guardrails, barriers and medians
• bollards and delineators
• traffic cones and temporary barriers
• street lights and traffic cameras
• variable message signs and gantries
• bridges and tunnels

PASS 4 — TRAFFIC PARTICIPANTS
Inspect:
• cars, taxis, vans, buses and trucks
• motorcycles and bicycles
• pedestrians, cyclists and riders
• animals

PASS 5 — FINAL SEARCH
Search again for small, distant or partially occluded signs, supplementary
plates, traffic lights, lane arrows, faded markings, delineators, bollards
and temporary traffic devices.

Only generate JSON after all passes are complete.

==================================================
YOLOPv2 OWNERSHIP
==================================================

YOLOPv2 is the auxiliary detector for these generic road-user classes:

• person
• rider
• car
• truck
• bus
• train
• lane markings (NOT crosswalks, arrows, diagonal patches or chevrons)
• motorcycle
• bicycle

Traffic lights and traffic signs are NOT imported from YOLOPv2 into the
final auxiliary detection set. They remain with the VLM / Locate Anything
branch.

Specialized vehicle identities also remain with the VLM / Locate Anything
branch when visually supported, including:
• police car
• taxi
• ambulance
• fire truck
• tow truck
• service vehicle
• delivery vehicle

YOLOPv2 does not own traffic infrastructure such as guardrails, bollards,
delineators, cones, street lights, gantries, bridges or tunnels.

This ownership split prevents duplicate generic road-user detections while
preserving traffic-specific objects outside YOLOPv2's closed set.

Do not assign ontology IDs from this section.

==================================================
SEMANTIC INTERPRETATION
==================================================

Use semantic interpretation ONLY when the identity is visually unambiguous
and supported by CLEAR, DIRECT evidence.

If a specific identity is uncertain, use a generic physical description.

Example:
A clearly recognizable STOP sign may be called a stop sign, but a
rectangular board with unreadable text should remain a
"rectangular traffic sign with unreadable text."

Do NOT infer meaning from:
• shape or colour alone
• text layout alone
• mounting position
• road location
• surrounding objects
• common traffic-sign conventions
• similarity to ontology classes

Do not claim a sign is bilingual, directional, information, route,
one-way, warning, regulatory, mandatory or prohibition unless visually
unmistakable.

Never invent text, symbols, arrows, languages, countries, rules or unseen
properties.

The description must answer:
"What can I actually see?"
not:
"What does this object probably mean?"

Do NOT assign the final ontology class or class ID.

==================================================
UNCERTAINTY AND SIGNAL STATE
==================================================

Describe ONLY what is directly visible. Use "unknown" when an attribute
cannot be confirmed.

For every clearly visible traffic signal:
• inspect each visible signal head
• identify the ACTIVE signal state when the illuminated lens is visible
• preserve the active colour and lens position explicitly

Do NOT infer hidden lenses, hidden heads, unseen symbols or invisible states.

If a red, yellow or green lens is visibly illuminated, that colour MUST be
preserved as direct evidence.

If the bottom lens is visibly green, explicitly describe an active green
state. Do not mark it unknown.

If visible signal heads have different active states, preserve those
differences as separate observations when relevant.

Only return a traffic signal when its head is clearly visible. A distant
coloured light alone is insufficient evidence.

==================================================
TRAFFIC SIGN TEXT AND SYMBOLS
==================================================

If visible text cannot be read:
"text": "unreadable"

If a visible pictogram cannot be identified:
"symbol": "unreadable"

If no text is visible:
"text": "none"

If no symbol is visible:
"symbol": "none"

Never confuse "none", "unknown" and "unreadable".

Unreadable text does NOT make the entire sign type unreadable.

If multiple scripts or visually distinct writing systems are visible,
describe that visual fact without inventing their languages.

==================================================
OBJECT GROUP
==================================================

Every object must have exactly one object_group.

Use ONLY:
vehicle
road_user
animal
traffic_sign
traffic_signal
road_marking
road_infrastructure
infrastructure
temporary_object
country_specific

object_group is the BROAD PHYSICAL CATEGORY, not the ontology class.

Examples:
bus/car/truck → vehicle
pedestrian/cyclist/rider → road_user
traffic sign → traffic_sign
traffic light → traffic_signal
crosswalk/lane divider/road arrow → road_marking
guardrail/bollard/delineator/median → road_infrastructure

Do NOT use fine-grained ontology class names as object_group.

Use "road_user" for people and human road participants.
Do NOT use "person" as object_group.

==================================================
DESCRIPTION
==================================================

The description is the PRIMARY source of visual evidence.

Write ONE concise, information-rich description of approximately 30–70 words.
Describe ONLY observable characteristics.

Where applicable include:
• colour, shape and approximate size
• active signal lens colour/position
• border and background
• pictograms, arrows and readable text
• reflective appearance
• material only if visually obvious
• mounting
• distinctive markings
• damage or partial occlusion

For signs, describe geometry, border, background, pictogram, arrow, text,
language if visually identifiable, supplementary plates and mounting.

For road markings, describe solid/dashed pattern, single/double lines,
direction, symbols, continuity, wear and orientation.

Preserve direct visual evidence FIRST. Add semantic interpretation only
when identity is visually unambiguous.

CONFIDENCE:
• "confidence" must be a number from 0.0 to 1.0.
• It represents confidence that the description and semantic identity are
  visually supported.
• It is NOT localization confidence or ontology confidence.
• Use lower values when identity is uncertain.
• Do not invent certainty when multiple classes remain plausible.
